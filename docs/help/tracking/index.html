Tracking Settings Help

Tune how DayMotion decides you stopped.

Tracking settings control how movement becomes a route, how pauses become stops, and how much detail DayMotion records while balancing battery use.

What The Controls Do

Each setting changes how DayMotion interprets movement.

Auto-detect stops

Lets DayMotion create stops automatically when your location settles inside the dwell circle long enough.

Show activity events

Shows started, paused, resumed, and stopped rows so the timeline explains recording state changes.

Battery saver mode

Reduces GPS polling frequency. This can save battery but may make routes and stop timing less detailed.

Display units

Changes how distances and weather values appear across journeys.

Dwell Circle

A stop needs both a radius and enough time.

DayMotion watches for a dwell pattern: your location remains inside a circle around roughly the same place for a selected duration. The circle is controlled by Stop radius. The time requirement is controlled by Stop duration.

Stop radius

The size of the dwell circle. A smaller radius asks DayMotion to be stricter about staying near one place. A larger radius tolerates more GPS drift or small movement nearby.

Stop duration

How long you need to remain inside the dwell circle before the pause becomes a stop.

Both must match

A brief pause inside the circle is not enough. A long pause while wandering outside the circle is not enough either.

GPS drift matters

Buildings, trees, transit, and weak signal can make location points move even when you are standing still. A larger radius can absorb that drift.

Examples

How radius and duration change stop capture.

Small radius, short duration

Captures more precise, more frequent stops. Useful for walking days, parks, waterfronts, and compact areas.

Small radius, long duration

Captures fewer precise stops. Useful when you only want places where you truly stayed.

Large radius, short duration

Captures pauses more easily. Useful when GPS is noisy, but it can create extra stops around traffic lights, parking, or slow movement.

Large radius, long duration

Creates calmer stop detection for driving or longer visits, but may merge nearby places into one stop.

Tips

Start with the kind of journey you record most.

Walking

Use a smaller radius if you care about individual places. Increase duration if short pauses are becoming stops.

Driving

Use a larger radius or longer duration to avoid turning traffic and parking maneuvers into separate stops.

Noisy GPS areas

If stops fail to appear even when you stayed put, increase the radius before increasing duration.

Too many stops

Increase duration first. If nearby places are still fragmented, increase radius next.

Questions

Common questions

What is the dwell circle?

It is the stop radius around a place. DayMotion looks for location points that stay inside that circle long enough to count as a stop.

Why did DayMotion miss a stop?

The pause may have been shorter than Stop duration, outside the Stop radius, affected by GPS drift, or recorded while tracking was paused.

Why did DayMotion create too many stops?

The duration may be too short, the radius may be too small for GPS drift, or slow movement may look like repeated pauses.

Does battery saver change stop detection?

It can. Lower polling frequency may reduce route detail and make stop timing less exact, especially during short visits.

Tracking settings affect how precise journey routes and stops become. Choose values that match the level of detail you want recorded.
